  1. INTRODUCTION

                This Operating Instruction sets out the official guidelines governing
                the implementation and management of the Grievance Mechanism
                within OERNL's operational and community engagement framework.
                It replaces any prior versions that referenced ENI systems or local
                operating instructions and applies across all OERNL operational
                areas.

This document is the property of Oando. All rights reserved.                           4
OPI| Handling Stakeholders Grievances

  2. OBJECTIVES

                 The objective of this Operating Instruction is to provide a structured
                 mechanism for the effective management and resolution of concerns
                 or complaints that may arise between OERNL and local stakeholders
                 in the company's operational areas. It aims to promote transparency,
                 fairness, and consistency in addressing grievances while minimizing
                 risks to the Company's reputation and maintaining positive
                 community relations.

                 An effectively implemented grievance mechanism will support the
                 company by:

                  · defining an effective stakeholder engagement strategy for
                       addressing grievance and concerns arising from our impacts
                       caused by company's operations or contractors working for the
                       company;

                  · maintaining social acceptance from local stakeholders by
                       demonstrating that the company takes community concerns
                       seriously.

                  · addressing grievances arising from the environmental, social and
                       health impacts of company's operations and the risks associated
                       with projects;

                  · assuring company's compliance with National and International
                       environmental and social standards; and

                  · assisting the company in fulfilling its responsibility to respect human
                      rights and promote shared prosperity.

  3. SCOPE OF APPLICATION

              This Operating Instruction applies to all grievances raised by individuals,
              groups, or communities affected by OERNL operations. It covers both individual
              and collective grievances and provides a fair, transparent, and accessible
              process for their management.

              Grievances excluded from this procedure include matters related to internal
              labour, employee welfare, or procurement processes, which shall be addressed
              through their respective company procedures.

              The grievance mechanism shall cover, but is not limited to, the following areas
              of concern:

                             1. Environmental Concerns: Complaints arising from oil spills or
                                 contamination of land and water bodies, including those affecting
                                 agriculture, fishing, and community health, as well as grievances
                                 over non-payment of relief materials to affected communities.

                             2. Gas Flaring: Issues relating to the health and environmental
                                 impacts of gas flaring, including air pollution and its contribution
                                 to climate change.

                             3. Land Degradation: Complaints concerning deforestation, soil
                                 erosion, or loss of land quality linked to OERNL's operations.

                             4. Community Displacement: Grievances related to displacement
                                 or resettlement of communities due to land acquisition for oil and
                                 gas infrastructure.

                             5. Cultural Heritage: Concerns regarding impacts on cultural sites,
                                 traditions, and practices affected by OERNL's operational
                                 activities.

                             6. Compensation: Disputes over adequacy, timeliness, or fairness
                                 of compensation for land use, environmental damage, or other
                                 community losses.

                             7. Local Employment: Complaints regarding limited job
                                 opportunities for host communities or perceived bias in hiring
                                 practices favouring non-indigenes.

                             8. Community Development Projects: Issues concerning the
                                 delivery, quality, or fairness of community projects, including
                                 exclusion from tender processes or denial of contract awards.

                             9. Community Recognition: Grievances arising from the non-
                                 recognition of a community, its Paramount Ruler, or Community
                                 Development Committee (CDC).

                             10. Delayed Payments: Complaints related to delays in payment to
                                 community contractors or workers engaged in company-related
                                 activities.

6. Activities and Operating Practices

         6.1 General Principles

                 OERNL recognizes that oil and gas activities can impact communities
                 socially and environmentally. A clear grievance mechanism builds trust and
                 demonstrates accountability to stakeholders. The grievance process shall
                 be accessible, legitimate, transparent, and based on continuous
                 improvement and rights-compatibility.

                 This grievance mechanism has been developed taking into account the
                 following general principles, aligned with international best practices:

                 a) Accessibility: The grievance process shall be made known and
                         accessible to all local stakeholders, including those who may face
                         challenges such as language barriers, literacy levels, financial
                         constraints, distance, or fear of reprisal.

                b) Continuous Improvement: The mechanism shall enable the
                         Company to capture lessons learned, enhance performance, and
                         prevent recurrence of similar grievances through adaptive
                         management.

                c) Engagement and dialogue: The procedure shall promote open
                         communication and constructive dialogue with individuals and local
                         stakeholders affected or likely to be affected by the Company's
                         operations, seeking mutually agreeable solutions rather than
                         unilateral actions.

                d) Equitability: The mechanism shall ensure fair access to information,
                         advice, and expertise required for balanced and equitable grievance
                         resolution.

                e) Legitimacy: The grievance structure shall be supported by clear
                         governance and accountability frameworks that foster transparency
                         and build stakeholder confidence.

                f) Predictability: The mechanism shall offer clarity regarding process
                         steps, expected timelines, and potential outcomes, along with a
                         defined plan for monitoring implementation.

                g) Rights-Compatibility: All outcomes and remedies arising from

                             grievance assessments shall align with internationally recognized

                             human rights standards.

                    h) Transparency: The process and outcomes of the grievance
                             mechanism shall be documented and communicated transparently to
                             strengthen trust and community confidence.

                        · Defining an effective stakeholder engagement strategy;

  6.2 Grievance Process and Timing

All grievances shall be handled with equal levels of integrity, confidentiality, and
respect, in full compliance with this Operating Instruction.

Where grievance is considered sensitive in nature, such as one that may disrupt
operations, escalate community tensions, or carry material implications, the
Grievance Owner or Grievance Coordinator shall immediately notify the Division
Manager, Stakeholders Management & Community Development (XMD). Any
grievance that may adversely affect OERNL's reputation at a national or
international level shall be promptly escalated to Company Management through
the General Manager, Base & District Management (GMD).

The grievance resolution process formally begins when a complaint is registered
with designated Company personnel within the relevant operational jurisdiction,

             6.3 Grievance Classification
                  Grievances received through the Company's Grievance Mechanism shall be
                  classified according to their level of severity to ensure appropriate handling,
                  escalation, and resolution. The categories are as follows:

                  1. Low Severity:

                  Grievances relating to incidents or damages, whether occurring, ongoing, or
                  potential, that have a limited impact and can be independently verified by third
                  parties. Examples include (non-exhaustive):

                        · Minor damage to property or livelihood activities that does not impair
                              overall usability or functionality (e.g., proven damage to private
                              assets resulting from construction activities).

                        · Alleged failure to comply with agreements or commitments made to
                              individuals or small groups.

                  2. Medium severity:

                       Grievances involving environmental or social impacts, real or perceived, with
                       short to medium-term effects on groups of individuals, broader livelihood
                       activities, or community resources. Examples include (non-exhaustive):

                        · Interference with the activities of groups of fishermen or farmers that
                              was not foreseen or compensated.

                        · Localized environmental disturbances that affect multiple
                              households or community members.

                      3. High severity:

                           Grievances involving actual or potential significant adverse impacts on
                           individuals, groups, or communities, especially those relating to
                           safety, wellbeing, rights, or serious disruptions to community life. This
                           category includes (non-exhaustive):

                           Human Rights-Related Grievances:
                           Cases involving violence, threats, intimidation, harassment,
                           aggression, or other serious harm to individuals or groups within host
                           communities, directly or indirectly linked to company activities or those

                             personnel (public or private).

                        · Complaints related to acts that cause, or may cause, physical,
                             psychological, sexual, or economic harm to women or girls in the
                             community, including threats, coercion, exploitation, or deprivation of
                             liberty in either public or private settings.

             6.4 Local Stakeholder Analysis and Prioritization

             The identification and prioritization of stakeholders involve gathering relevant
             information about each stakeholder or stakeholder group, including their status
             within the area, their level of influence or potential impact on OERNL operations,
             and their perceived understanding of the Company's current or planned
             activities.

             Following identification, each stakeholder is assessed based on two key criteria:

                        · Disposition: The stakeholder's attitude toward OERNL's operations and
                             objectives.

                        · Relevance (Power/Influence): The stakeholder's ability to influence
                             operational or strategic outcomes.

             The following simple, qualitative description are used to assess disposition and
             power/influence:
             Assessment of Disposition

             Disposition reflects the stakeholder's general attitude towards the Company and may
             be assessed using the following qualitative scale:

                        · Positive: stakeholders with constructive attitude towards the Company
                             and its operations.

                        · Moderately positive: stakeholders with averagely constructive attitude.
                        · Neutral: indifferent.
                        · Moderately negative: stakeholders are averagely hostile towards the

                             Company and its operations.
                        · Negative: stakeholders with hostile attitude towards the Company and its

                             operations.

             Assessment of Relevance (Power/Influence)

             Relevance measures the stakeholder's ability to influence Company activities or
             decisions and is assessed on a scale from low (1­2) to high (5­6):

             High (5­6): Stakeholders with significant influence over the Company's license
             to operate or substantial authority in decision-making, including regulatory
             authorities, national institutions, natural rulers, youth bodies, and community
             development committees.

             Medium (3­4): Stakeholder groups or representatives who can influence
             strategic decision-making (e.g., community associations) or individuals/small
             groups with influence over operational decisions (e.g., women's groups).

             Low (1­2): Stakeholders with limited or no capacity to influence Company
             decisions (e.g., communities outside OERNL operational areas).

            Stakeholder disposition and relevance may evolve over time. Therefore, the
            stakeholder analysis shall be reviewed and updated regularly to ensure it reflects
            current realities and engagement needs.

             6.5 Critical, Strategic, Intermediate and Marginal Stakeholders

                         Local stakeholders are categorized into four (4) groups based on their
                         Relevance (Power/Influence) and Disposition toward OERNL operations. These
                         categories support informed planning, prioritization, and targeted engagement.

                  1. Critical Stakeholders:

                       Stakeholders with high Relevance ( 4) and a negative Disposition
                       toward OERNL's activities or projects. These stakeholders require
                       priority, proactive, and frequent engagement to address concerns,
                       reduce potential risks, and gradually shift their disposition to a less critical
                       position. Their influence makes timely engagement essential to
                       preventing operational or reputational challenges.

                  2. Strategic Stakeholders

                       Stakeholders with high Relevance ( 4) and a non-negative Disposition
                       toward OERNL. These stakeholders must be closely monitored and
                       regularly consulted to ensure they remain informed of OERNL's projects,
                       initiatives, and decisions.
                       Failure to adequately address their requests or concerns could shift their
                       attitude negatively, creating operational or reputational risks.
                       Strong relationships with this group are essential, as they can support
                       development opportunities and help facilitate engagement with other
                       stakeholders.

                  3. Intermediate Stakeholders
                      Stakeholders with Relevance between 3 and < 4, regardless of
                      Disposition.

                      These stakeholders should be periodically monitored and engaged, as
                      both their influence and disposition can change over time, potentially
                      moving them into either the Strategic or Critical categories.

                  4. Marginal Stakeholders

                      Stakeholders with low Relevance (< 3), irrespective of their Disposition.
                      While they have limited influence over OERNL's decision-making, their
                      perceptions should be reviewed annually to assess any changes in
                      influence, networks, or community dynamics.
                      Engagement with this group may occur as needed, but they generally do
                      not require priority attention.

  6.7 Roles and Responsibilities

    6.7.1 Grievance Coordinator

The Grievance Coordinator is responsible for the overall coordination and oversight
of the grievance mechanism. Key responsibilities include:

             · Managing the grievance database and ensuring the integrity of all
                  records.

             · Serving as the primary focal point for coordination between relevant
                  departments.

             · Ensuring that resolution actions are completed and that grievance
                  handling remains effective and consistent.

             · Leading the internal grievance working group and liaising with senior
                  management as necessary.

             · Tracking, analyzing, and reporting grievance trends and performance
                  indicators.

             · Monitoring the progress of individual grievances to ensure timely
                  closure.

             · Providing monthly and quarterly grievance data summaries to the
                  XMD.

    6.7.2 Community Investment and Sustainability Relations

This function is responsible for ensuring alignment of the grievance mechanism with
sustainability standards and best practice. Responsibilities include:

             · Identifying opportunities for improvement and supporting periodic
                  review and update of this Operating Instruction.

             · Ensuring the overall integrity and credibility of the grievance
                  management system.

             · Ensuring that the mechanism is consistent with international best
                  practice and effectively communicated to internal stakeholders.

             · Monitoring compliance with the grievance process across all
                  operational areas.

             · Providing grievance data summaries and analysis when required.

    6.7.3 General Manager, District Management (GMD)

GMD provides senior-level oversight to ensure the effectiveness and credibility of the
grievance mechanism. Key responsibilities include:

             · Approving this grievance procedure and subsequent revisions.
             · Ensuring appropriate attention is given to thorough investigation and

                  resolution of grievances.
             · Managing decision-making processes for grievances involving third

                  parties that may materially impact Company reputation or require legal
                  review.

6.7.4  Stakeholders Management & Community Development Division
       Manager (XMD)

The XMD is responsible for oversight, resourcing, and quality assurance of the
grievance mechanism. Responsibilities include:

       · Ensuring all complaints are routed and coordinated through the
           designated Grievance Owner.

       · Providing adequate resources (personnel, systems, procedures, budget)
           for effective grievance management.

       · Assigning complaints to the appropriate Grievance Owner and ensuring
           periodic reporting.

       · Ensuring that investigations are completed within expected timeframes.

       · Reviewing and endorsing proposed grievance resolutions.

       · Supporting monitoring and evaluation of the grievance mechanism.

       · Ensuring that internal and external reporting requirements are met.

       · Ensuring relevant personnel receive adequate training to perform
           assigned responsibilities.

             6.8 Monitoring and Reporting

          Monitoring compliance with this Procedure and reporting on grievance-related activities
          form part of the Company's annual continuous improvement process. This ensures the
          responsiveness, integrity, and overall effectiveness of the grievance mechanism.

          On a quarterly basis, Management shall undertake a structured review focused on the
          following:

                        · The extent to which the Local Stakeholders Grievance Mechanism is
                             being implemented as designed.

                        · Verification that grievances are being correctly classified according to
                             defined categories and severity levels.

                        · Review of data and key performance indicators, including:
                                 a. Total number of grievances received, classified by level and type.
                                 b. Number of open grievances, classified by level and type.
                                 c. Total number of resolved grievances, classified by level and type.
                                 d. Number of grievances resolved through rapid response.
                                 e. Identification of recurring or systemic grievances associated with
                                      specific departments or operational areas.

                         · Trend analysis to determine whether grievances are concentrated around
                             particular issues, locations, or incidents.

                         · Recommendations for corrective and preventive measures to address any
                             deficiencies identified during the review.

               6.8.1 Periodic Review of the Grievance Mechanism

          The Handling Community Grievance Mechanism shall be formally reviewed every four
          (4) years as part of the broader Sustainability Management System review, in alignment
          with the requirements of the Upstream and Central Sustainability Functions. The review
          may be triggered or informed by:

                        · Trends and changes observed in key performance indicators.
                        · Findings from internal audits conducted to assess the effectiveness and

                             efficiency of the mechanism.
                        · Feedback obtained through stakeholder engagement processes or from

                             the practical handling of grievances, particularly with respect to:
                                 a. Stakeholder awareness of the grievance mechanism.
                                 b. Levels of trust and confidence in the mechanism.
                                 c. Suggestions or opportunities for improvement.
